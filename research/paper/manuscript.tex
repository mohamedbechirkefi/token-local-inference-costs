\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Running language models locally trades answer quality against latency
and memory. The sources consulted for this study (a preliminary,
non-systematic review) do not report task accuracy and resource use
together, paired at the item level, on one consumer machine. This study
asked how local model configurations trade off quality, latency and
resource use on one Apple M1 Pro laptop (16 GB) running Ollama 0.9.6.
Three experiments were preregistered and frozen by the owner before any
model output was seen. Two of them failed before sending any request:
the installed runtime cannot render the chat template of one required
model (ministral-3:8b), and the frozen protocol aborts a run when a
model cannot be described. The primary comparison of model sizes
(EXP-001) and the cold-start study (EXP-002) therefore produced no
results. The third experiment (EXP-003) compared Llama 3.1 8B at Q8\_0
and Q4\_K\_M on a fixed random subset of 300 ARC-Challenge test items,
five greedy repetitions each (3,000 requests, no failures). Strict
accuracy was 0.733 against 0.717: a difference of +1.67 percentage
points, 95 \% paired bootstrap interval {[}-1.00, +4.33{]}, exact
McNemar p = 0.332. No difference was detected; because no equivalence
margin was frozen, this is not evidence of equivalence. Q8\_0 used 56 \%
more runtime-reported model memory (9,162.4 against 5,872.0 MiB) and
decoded more slowly (40.1 against 53.9 tokens/s), yet its median
end-to-end latency was lower (437.7 against 477.5 ms), because these
two-token answers are dominated by prefill. The findings hold for this
machine, runtime, benchmark and prompt template only.

\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

Local inference on personal hardware moves the cost of a request from a
provider's fee to the user's own memory, time and electricity, and
quantized model files let 8-billion-parameter models run on a 16 GB
laptop, as this study's runs show. The choice of a quantization level is
usually made from file size and throughput tables (for example the
llama.cpp quantization table, llamacpp-quantize) or from aggregate
scaling results on datacentre hardware (dettmers2022kbit). What the
sources consulted here do not give a user of one laptop is a paired
measurement of task accuracy together with the latency and memory cost,
at the item level and with uncertainty, on that laptop.

This study set out to provide such a measurement for the configurations
installed on one machine. The research question (master prompt section
10) is: how do local model configurations trade off quality, latency and
resource use? It was translated into three frozen experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{EXP-001 (primary):} do llama3.1:8b (A1) and ministral-3:8b
  (A2) differ in accuracy from the smallest installed instruction model,
  phi4-mini:3.8b (A0), all at Q4\_K\_M, and at what cost? H0\_k:
  Delta\_k = 0; H1\_k: Delta\_k != 0, Holm-corrected over two contrasts.
\item
  \textbf{EXP-002:} is the runtime-cold time-to-first-token penalty
  larger for larger model files (A0 \textless{} A1 \textless{} A2)?
\item
  \textbf{EXP-003:} for Llama 3.1 8B, does accuracy differ between Q8\_0
  and Q4\_K\_M, and what are the latency and memory costs of the higher
  precision? H0: Delta = 0; H1: Delta != 0.
\end{itemize}

The actual contributions are narrower than the plan, and are stated as
such:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  A preregistered, paired comparison of Q8\_0 and Q4\_K\_M for one 8B
  model on one laptop, reporting accuracy with an item-level interval
  alongside runtime-reported memory, latency, time to first token and
  throughput (EXP-003).
\item
  A documented negative outcome: two frozen experiments failed before
  any request because of a runtime and model-template incompatibility,
  with the failure mode, its detection point and the options left to the
  owner (EXP-001, EXP-002).
\item
  An evidence pipeline in which every number in this manuscript maps to
  a committed result file, including an exploratory re-analysis that
  found and quantified a warm-up artefact in the runner.
\end{enumerate}

No novelty is claimed beyond this scope.

\section{2. Related work}\label{related-work}

The literature review (\texttt{research/literature\_review.md}) is
preliminary and not systematic; every source below was opened on
2026-11-24, and for arXiv papers only the abstract page was read.

\textbf{Quantization and accuracy.} Dettmers and Zettlemoyer
(dettmers2022kbit; abstract only) report, from more than 35,000
experiments on models from 19M to 176B parameters, that 4-bit precision
is generally optimal for the trade-off between total model bits and
zero-shot accuracy. Dettmers et al.~(dettmers2022int8; abstract only)
report that 8-bit inference roughly halves memory without performance
degradation. Lin et al.~(lin2023awq; abstract only) describe
activation-aware weight-only quantization. These results concern
quantization methods and scaling across many models; they do not measure
the GGUF k-quant files used here on a laptop.

\textbf{Throughput tables.} The llama.cpp quantization documentation
(llamacpp-quantize; page opened) lists, for Llama-3.1-8B, Q4\_K\_M at
4.58 GiB and 71.93 tokens/s and Q8\_0 at 7.95 GiB and 50.93 tokens/s.
The hardware for that table was not recorded in our notes, and it
reports no task accuracy. These values are external reference points and
are never mixed with our measurements.

\textbf{Multi-metric and energy evaluation.} HELM (liang2022helm;
abstract only) includes efficiency among the measured dimensions of
language-model evaluation. Samsi et al.~(samsi2023watts; abstract only)
benchmark energy and performance of LLaMA models on V100 and A100 GPUs.
Both concern datacentre-scale settings.

\textbf{Prompt sensitivity.} Sclar et al.~(sclar2023format; abstract
only) report accuracy differences of up to 76 points from formatting
changes alone and recommend reporting a range over formats. This
motivated sensitivity analysis S1, which was not run (Section 5.4).

\textbf{Position of this study.} This is a small replication-style
measurement in a new setting (one consumer laptop, one runtime), not an
extension of any method. It adds paired item-level accuracy to the kind
of cost figures that tool documentation reports.

\section{3. Background and formal
model}\label{background-and-formal-model}

Let i = 1, \ldots, n index the benchmark items (n = 300) and r = 0,
\ldots, 4 the repetitions. For arm k, let y\_ikr in \{0, 1\} be the
strict correctness of the answer extracted from the response. Accuracy
uses the per-item mean over repetitions,

c\_ik = (1/5) sum\_r y\_ikr, Acc\_k = (1/n) sum\_i c\_ik,

and the primary estimand for a candidate arm k against baseline 0 is the
paired difference

Delta\_k = (1/n) sum\_i (c\_ik - c\_i0).

The exact McNemar test uses majority correctness m\_ik = 1{[}c\_ik
\textgreater= 0.5{]}; with five repetitions no tie occurs. The
discordant counts are b = \#\{i : m\_ik = 1, m\_i0 = 0\} and c = \#\{i :
m\_ik = 0, m\_i0 = 1\}, and the two-sided exact p-value is min(1, 2
sum\_\{j \textless= min(b, c)\} C(b + c, j) / 2\^{}(b + c)).

For timing, each item's five end-to-end latencies are reduced to their
median L\_ik, and arms are summarised by the median and quartiles of
L\_ik over items. Decode throughput is eval\_count / eval\_duration x
1e9 tokens/s and prefill throughput is prompt\_eval\_count /
prompt\_eval\_duration x 1e9 tokens/s, both from the runtime's own
counters (field definitions in ollama-api) and both summarised as
medians over records. The named memory metric (amendment F7) is the
runtime-reported model size from \texttt{/api/ps} at the end of each
block, maximised over blocks, in MiB (2\^{}20 bytes).

The mechanism stated in the EXP-003 protocol (item 2) is that higher
precision reduces quantization error but increases the bytes read per
generated token, lowering decode throughput on bandwidth-bound hardware.
This mechanism is a stated expectation, not a verified property of this
machine.

\section{4. Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{4.1 Design and protocol
registration}\label{design-and-protocol-registration}

The main protocol (\texttt{research/protocol.md}) and the three
experiment protocols were drafted at M0, amended with nine
owner-approved amendments (F1 to F9), and frozen by the owner on
2026-11-24 (G-FREEZE; decision DL-008). Their SHA-256 values are
recorded in \texttt{research/freeze.json}; the runner refuses a
confirmatory config unless the protocol, config and item-file hashes
match. No real model output and no ARC item had been seen before the
freeze (freeze record, C4). The EXP-002 runner mode and config were
added under the owner's second-round approval (DL-009), and the EXP-002
penalty analysis was committed before any EXP-002 output (DL-010). All
three experiments are confirmatory; the smoke runs and the M4
re-analysis are exploratory.

\subsection{4.2 Data}\label{data}

ARC-Challenge (clark2018arc), test split, 1,172 items, licence CC BY-SA
4.0 (dataset card arc-hf), downloaded from the Hugging Face repository
\texttt{allenai/ai2\_arc} at revision 210d026f. A simple random sample
of 300 items without replacement was drawn once with seed 20261124; the
item IDs and the SHA-256 of the source file are committed
(\texttt{data/arc\_challenge\_test\_subset\_ids.json},
\texttt{data/manifest.json}), the question texts are not, and
\texttt{scripts/fetch\_arc.py} regenerates them. No tuning was done on
these items. A chance floor (the mean of 1 / number of options) is 0.251
for this subset.

\subsection{4.3 Configurations and
baseline}\label{configurations-and-baseline}

All arms ran in Ollama 0.9.6 on 127.0.0.1. EXP-001 and EXP-002 used A0
phi4-mini:3.8b (baseline, 2.49 GB), A1 llama3.1:8b (4.92 GB) and A2
ministral-3:8b (6.02 GB), all GGUF Q4\_K\_M. EXP-003 used
\texttt{llama3.1:8b} (Q4\_K\_M, baseline) and
\texttt{llama3.1:8b-instruct-q8\_0} (Q8\_0, pulled 2026-11-25, manifest
digest b158ded7\ldots, 8,540,789,934 bytes of layers; JRN-0005).
qwen3.5:4b was excluded by the owner (F4). The owner accepted the Llama
3.1 Community License for this study (F6).

\subsection{4.4 Measurement}\label{measurement}

Each request used the zero-shot template T1 (answer with the label
only), temperature 0, num\_predict 8, num\_ctx 2048 and runtime seeds 0
to 4 for the five repetitions. Item order within an arm and arm order
within a repetition were shuffled with seed 1000 + repetition,
identically for both arms. Each arm block began with one discarded
warm-up request. The client measured end-to-end latency and time to
first token with \texttt{time.perf\_counter\_ns} on the streamed
response. Per arm, the run manifest records the SHA-256 of the chat
template, default system prompt, default parameters and Modelfile
reported by \texttt{/api/show} (F8). A 10 Hz sampler recorded the
resident memory of Ollama processes, system memory and swap. Energy was
to be modelled from wall-clock time and an assumed power range (F5); no
energy was measured.

The M4 re-analysis found that the discarded warm-up request uses the
block's first item (\texttt{runner.run\_matrix}), so the first measured
request of every block repeats a prompt the runtime has just processed
(Section 5.4). The frozen protocol does not specify the warm-up prompt.

\subsection{4.5 Statistical analysis}\label{statistical-analysis}

Paired bootstrap over items (10,000 resamples, seed 7, percentile 95 \%
intervals) for Delta, and the exact McNemar test. EXP-003 has a single
contrast and no multiplicity correction; EXP-001 would have used Holm
over two. The frozen contradiction rule: H1 is not supported if the 95
\% interval contains 0 and the test does not reject at alpha 0.05, and
this outcome is reported as ``no detected difference, never as
equivalence'' (main protocol section 2). \textbf{No equivalence margin
is defined in any frozen protocol}, so no equivalence test is possible.
Timing and memory are secondary and descriptive. The frozen protocol
also asks for a bootstrap interval for ``the median latency ratio of
each arm to the baseline''; the analysis code does not compute it and
the text does not say whether this is a ratio of medians or a median of
per-item ratios (DL-011 gap 1). Section 5.4 reports both readings as
exploratory values; the definition remains the owner's choice.

The planned sample size assumed a discordant-pair proportion of 0.25,
giving a 95 \% half-width of about 5.7 points and a minimum detectable
difference of about 9 points at 80 \% power (main protocol section 7).

\subsection{4.6 Reproducibility}\label{reproducibility}

Environment: \texttt{uv\ sync\ -\/-frozen} from \texttt{uv.lock} (Python
3.12, numpy 2.3.4, psutil 7.1.0, jsonschema 4.25.1). Data:
\texttt{python3\ -I\ scripts/fetch\_arc.py\ -\/-repo\ .}. Run:
\texttt{.venv/bin/token-lab\ run\ -\/-config\ experiments/EXP-003.json}.
Report, tables and figure:
\texttt{.venv/bin/token-lab\ report\ -\/-result\ results/confirmatory/RUN-EXP-003-0001/result.json}.
Exploratory re-analysis:

The confirmatory run used code revision

\subsection{5.1 EXP-001 (primary): failed, no
result}\label{exp-001-primary-failed-no-result}

The run passed the freeze guard and the item checks, then aborted at the
per-arm \texttt{/api/show} fingerprint required by F8: for
ministral-3:8b the runtime returned HTTP 500 ``template: :10: function
currentDate not defined'' (JRN-0007). No generation request was sent and
no result file was written. The same error had appeared for every A2
request in the exploratory smoke run RUN-SMOKE-0005 (JRN-0006), which
ran the morning of the confirmatory window; M0 could not detect it
because the server was not running then. The failure reflects runtime
compatibility, not model quality. \textbf{The study therefore has no
evidence on the primary question}: nothing is claimed about A0 versus A1
versus A2. The exploratory smoke run produced accuracy values for A0 and
A1 on 10 validation items; they were never intended as evidence and are
not reported as such.

\subsection{5.2 EXP-002: failed, no
result}\label{exp-002-failed-no-result}

EXP-002 aborted at the same step for the same reason (JRN-0007). No
cold-start penalty was measured and its ordering hypothesis is untested.
An exploratory smoke run of the cold/warm mechanism on A0 alone
(RUN-SMOKE-0006, a different item, a concurrent model download) is
evidence only that the unload-and-check mechanism works.

The owner's choice for EXP-001 and EXP-002 is pending (DL-011, DL-012):
(a) upgrade Ollama and run the frozen matrices unchanged, recording the
new runtime version; (b) a new freeze without A2 or with another arm;
(c) keep both recorded as failed. This manuscript decides nothing among
them.

\section{6. Discussion}\label{discussion}

\textbf{What the evidence supports.} On this laptop, moving Llama 3.1 8B
from Q4\_K\_M to Q8\_0 produced no detected accuracy difference on 300
ARC-Challenge items, at the price of about 3.2 GiB more runtime memory.
This must not be read as ``Q4\_K\_M is as accurate as Q8\_0'': the point
estimate favours Q8\_0 (11 against 6 discordant items), and the 95 \%
interval extends to a gain of 4.33 points. The interval's lower end
(-1.00 points) is a property of this 300-item subset and this bootstrap;
because no non-inferiority or equivalence margin was preregistered, it
supports no non-inferiority claim. The result is compatible with the
abstract-level statement of dettmers2022kbit that 4-bit precision is
generally near the best size and accuracy trade-off, but one model and
one benchmark do not test that statement.

\textbf{Cost.} The decode ratio of 0.744 has the same direction as the
ratio implied by the llama.cpp table (50.93 / 71.93, about 0.708). That
table comes from unrecorded hardware and settings, so the comparison is
a sanity check on direction, not a replication. The memory ratio (1.56)
is lower than the ratio of the model files (8.54 against 4.92 GB, about
1.74). One plausible reason is that the runtime-reported size also
includes the KV cache and other allocations at num\_ctx 2048, which
would be similar for both arms; the allocation was not decomposed, so
this is unverified.

\textbf{Why Q8\_0 was faster end to end.} With answers of two tokens,
latency is mostly prefill, and prefill was faster for Q8\_0 in every
prompt-length bin (E4). The protocol's mechanism predicts slower
\emph{decoding} for Q8\_0, which was observed; it does not predict
prefill. A kernel-efficiency difference between the Q8\_0 and Q4\_K\_M
code paths during the compute-bound prefill phase is one possible
explanation; it is not verified here. The latency ordering is expected
to reverse for longer generations, where decoding dominates, but that is
an inference, not a measurement.

\textbf{Competing explanations for the EXP-003 conclusions.} The
protocols name the following (main protocol section 12; EXP-003 item 4):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Different conversion pipelines or chat templates.} The template,
  system prompt and parameter hashes are identical between arms; the
  Modelfile hashes differ, as expected from different weight blobs. The
  provenance of the two weight files is otherwise not audited. Partly
  addressed.
\item
  \emph{Instruction-following and format compliance.} S2 gave the same
  contrast, and excluding the format-failure items (post hoc,
  exploratory) left a difference of the same sign and similar size.
  Addressed for T1.
\item
  \emph{Tokenizer effects.} Prompt token counts were identical for every
  item and repetition (E8): the arms share a tokenizer. Addressed.
\item
  \emph{Runtime scheduling and caching.} Arm order alternated between
  repetitions and position changed latency by less than 1 ms (E6). The
  warm-up artefact (E3) affects one record per block, the same item in
  both arms, and leaves the per-item medians unchanged. Prompt caching
  beyond that case was not tested. Partly addressed.
\item
  \emph{Background load on a shared laptop.} The run used an
  owner-scheduled quiet-machine window (start load average 2.29 after
  waiting for it to fall below 2.5, AC power, no other client of the
  session's own Ollama server), repetition medians varied with a
  coefficient of variation of 0.002, and drift was below 1 \%; about 3
  GiB of swap was already in use before the run. Partly addressed.
\item
  \emph{Training-data contamination.} Both arms are the same model at
  different precision, so contamination would not bias the paired
  contrast, but it limits what ARC accuracy says about quality. Not
  tested.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The two failures.} The F8 rule, added at the freeze so that each
arm's chat template would be hashed, made the failure visible before any
request instead of letting the run proceed with one arm returning
errors. The cost is that the primary question is unanswered. The
experience suggests that a runtime compatibility check of every arm
belongs in the feasibility audit, before the freeze; in this study it
could not happen because the runtime was not running at M0.

\section{7. Limitations and threats to
validity}\label{limitations-and-threats-to-validity}

The full list is in \texttt{research/limitations.md}. The most
important:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{No result on the primary question.} EXP-001 and EXP-002
  failed; the only confirmatory result concerns two quantizations of one
  model, an experiment the plan called optional.
\item
  \textbf{Not equivalence.} No equivalence margin was frozen, so the
  EXP-003 interval is only an interval. Differences of a few points are
  compatible with the data.
\item
  \textbf{Power and its assumption.} The design assumed a discordant
  proportion of 0.25 and a minimum detectable difference of about 9
  points. The observed discordance was much lower (17 of 300) and the
  interval correspondingly narrower (half-width 2.67 against a planned
  5.66 points), but that does not create an equivalence test after the
  fact.
\item
  \textbf{One template.} S1 was not run; prompt format can change
  accuracy substantially (sclar2023format).
\item
  \textbf{One machine, one runtime version, one benchmark, short
  answers.} Nothing generalises to other hardware, runtimes, tasks or
  generation lengths.
\item
  \textbf{Latency is prefill-dominated and descriptive.} The frozen
  latency-ratio interval is not implemented; the exploratory intervals
  cover within-session item variation only. Prefill rates depend on
  prompt length (E4).
\item
  \textbf{Memory metrics.} The named metric is the runtime's own
  accounting, not an independent measurement. Peak RSS was lower for
  Q8\_0 than for Q4\_K\_M and does not capture the model's Metal
  allocations; it is secondary only.
\item
  \textbf{Warm-up artefact.} Ten of 3,000 records were measured right
  after an identical warm-up prompt (E3).
\item
  \textbf{Energy.} No energy figure is reported; S4 lacks a frozen power
  range.
\item
  \textbf{Construct.} ARC-Challenge multiple-choice accuracy is a narrow
  proxy for quality; contamination is untested.
\end{itemize}

\section{9. Conclusion and future
work}\label{conclusion-and-future-work}

On one Apple M1 Pro laptop with Ollama 0.9.6, Llama 3.1 8B at Q8\_0
showed no detected accuracy difference from Q4\_K\_M on 300
ARC-Challenge items (+1.67 pp, 95 \% CI {[}-1.00, +4.33{]}), while using
56 \% more runtime-reported memory and decoding about a quarter more
slowly; for two-token answers its end-to-end latency was lower. The
study's primary comparison of model sizes and its cold-start experiment
produced no results because of a runtime and template incompatibility.
Feasible next steps, each needing the owner: resolve EXP-001 and EXP-002
(options a to c above); fix the definition of the latency-ratio
interval; freeze configs for S1 and an S4 power range; and, if smaller
accuracy differences matter, preregister an equivalence margin and a
larger item set.
